Four things that might surprise you

straighten

Stretched out in a straight line, your digestive system is about 30 feet long — longer than a school bus.

science

Your stomach acid is strong enough to dissolve a metal spoon — a slimy mucus lining keeps it from hurting you.

schedule

One bite of food can take one to three whole days to make its full trip through your body.

psychology

Your gut has millions of nerve cells and is sometimes called your "second brain" — that's where butterflies come from.

The full trip

Every stop your food makes

Professor Villi walked us through it — here's the journey, stage by stage.

Mouth
Teeth chop, saliva starts breaking food down before you even swallow.
Esophagus
Peristalsis squeezes food down in about 10 seconds — even upside down.
Stomach
Churns like a washing machine and melts food with powerful acid.
Small intestine
Tiny villi soak up nutrients and send them into your blood.
Large intestine
Soaks up leftover water — what's left becomes waste.
